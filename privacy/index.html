This privacy policy is applicable to the DogPulse app (hereinafter referred to as "Application") for Android and iPhone, which was developed by André Crous (hereinafter referred to as "Service Provider") as a Freemium service. This service is provided "AS IS".

What information does the Application obtain and how is it used?

The Application does not require registration. All data you enter, including dog profiles, daily health logs, weight records, photos, medications, and vet visit information, is stored locally on your device. It is not transmitted to or accessible by the Service Provider, except for the data sent when you choose to use an AI feature (see the section on Artificial Intelligence below).

Backups

There are two kinds of backup, and the Service Provider receives neither of them.

The backup file you export. In the Dogs tab you can export a backup file and choose where to save or send it, for example to your files, cloud storage or email. The file is created on your phone and goes only where you send it. It is a readable text file and is not encrypted, so keep it somewhere safe. It contains your dog profiles (including microchip, insurance details, allergies, contacts and date of passing), all your records (daily logs with symptoms, foods and notes, weights, foods, medications and doses given, vaccinations, vet visits, heat cycles and pregnancies), the names and sizes of vet visit documents, and your settings: goal weights, notification settings, and the country and city you typed for tick season alerts. It does not contain your photos or vet visit documents themselves, your AI or analytics choices, or your subscription.

Your phone's own backup. If device backup is turned on in your Android settings, Android may copy the Application's data, including photos and vet visit documents, to your own Google account and restore it when you reinstall or set up a new phone. Android only does this when the Application's data is under 25 MB, so with many photos or documents it may not be backed up. If iCloud Backup or a computer backup is turned on for your iPhone, iOS may include the Application's data in that backup. Google or Apple manages that copy under your account or on your computer. These backups depend on your settings and on Google or Apple, so the Service Provider cannot guarantee them. The backup file you export is the way to keep your own copy.

What information does the Application collect automatically?

The Application can use Firebase Analytics (a Google service) to collect usage data, such as which features are used and how often, to help improve the app. This is your choice. The first time you open a new installation of the Application, it asks whether you want to share anonymous usage data. Nothing is sent before you choose.

If you choose Share, the data collected includes a device identifier (the Firebase app instance ID), your anonymous purchase ID, general usage events (for example "onboarding step viewed", "first health log saved", "paywall viewed", "subscription started" or "PDF generated"), counts such as the number of days you have logged, and how many days after installation an event happened. Google also derives your device model, operating system version and approximate region from your IP address. If you choose No thanks, the Application does not send these usage events. If you installed DogPulse before this question was introduced, usage analytics stay on until you switch them off. Versions of the Application before 1.1.6 on Android could record a few basic automatic events, such as the app being opened, in the moments before you made your choice.

You can change your choice at any time in the Dogs tab under "Share usage analytics". The data you enter into the Application (dog profiles, dog names, health logs, notes, photos, etc.) is never part of this collection. You can find Google's privacy policy at policies.google.com/privacy.

The Application uses Firebase Crashlytics (a Google service) to receive crash and error reports. A report contains your device model, operating system version, app version, the screen where the error happened and technical details of the error. Crash reports are sent whatever you choose about usage analytics, and crash reporting cannot be switched off in the Application.

The Application uses RevenueCat to manage subscriptions. RevenueCat receives an anonymous purchase ID, your purchase and subscription status, and a record each time a subscription screen is shown, so the Service Provider can see how often the subscription offer is viewed. This happens whatever you choose about usage analytics. Only while analytics is on, RevenueCat also receives the Firebase app instance ID. Payment is handled by Google Play on Android and by the App Store on iPhone. The Service Provider never sees your card details.

Does the Application collect precise real-time location information of the device?

This Application does not gather precise information about the location of your mobile device.

If you use tick season alerts, the city or country you type in, and the coordinates found for it, are sent to Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com) to look up the weather. No account or identifier is sent with this request. To stop these requests, turn off tick season alerts.

Does the Application use Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies?

The Application uses Anthropic's Claude API to analyse dog health log data and generate pattern insights and vet visit briefs. This only happens when you tap an AI feature, and only after you have tapped Allow on a screen that explains what is sent and to whom. Until you tap Allow, nothing is sent. You can withdraw your permission at any time in the Dogs tab by turning off "AI insights data sharing". After that, nothing is sent until you allow it again.

When you ask for an insight or a brief, the request goes through a small proxy run by the Service Provider on Cloudflare, which checks your subscription with RevenueCat, applies daily limits and passes your dog's data on to Anthropic. The data is not linked to your name, email address or account, but it does include your dog's name and any text you typed yourself.

Sent to the proxy and on to Anthropic:

  • First, profile-only insight: your dog's name, breed, age, sex, neutered status and current weight, and no logs.
  • AI insights: your dog's name, breed, age and current weight, up to 200 log entries from the last 90 days (energy, appetite, mood, bathroom, water, symptoms, food, exercise) and the weight history.
  • Vet brief: your dog's name, any allergies or conditions you entered, 60 days of logs, 90 days of weights, active medications with dosage, schedule and start and end dates, 30 days of medication records (given, skipped or snoozed), and the visit date and reason.

Anything you typed into those fields is included, such as the dog's name, symptoms, food, allergies, the visit reason and medication names. Please do not type personal details about yourself there. Photos and the free-text notes fields are never sent.

Sent to the proxy only, not on to Anthropic: your anonymous purchase ID and a number identifying your dog in the app, used for daily limits and the subscription check.

What the proxy keeps:

  • Your IP address, as a request counter for 24 hours (at most 10 AI requests per IP address per day), and in operational logs.
  • Counters tied to your anonymous purchase ID for 24 hours, and a 60 second cache of your subscription status.
  • For the one-time free insight reward, your anonymous purchase ID and the date it was used, with no expiry, so the reward cannot be claimed twice.
  • It does not store your dog's data or the AI's answers.

Requests to the proxy and to Anthropic use encrypted HTTPS connections. Anthropic processes this data on the Service Provider's behalf under its commercial API terms. Those terms state that Anthropic does not train its models on this data, and Anthropic keeps it only for a limited period. Anthropic's privacy policy can be found at anthropic.com/privacy. AI-generated insights are observations only and do not constitute veterinary advice.

What are my opt-out rights?

You can stop all use of the Application at any time by uninstalling it. You may use the standard uninstall process available on your mobile device or via the app marketplace through which you downloaded the Application. You can also switch off usage analytics at any time in the Dogs tab under "Share usage analytics", withdraw your permission for AI data transfers in the Dogs tab under "AI insights data sharing" (or simply not use AI features), avoid weather lookups by not using tick season alerts, and turn off your phone's own backup for the Application in your Android or iPhone settings.

Why does the Service Provider process data?

  • Subscriptions: needed to provide the service you asked for.
  • Subscription screen views in RevenueCat: the Service Provider's legitimate interest in understanding how often the subscription offer is seen.
  • AI features: your consent, which you give on the screen shown before first use and can withdraw at any time in the Dogs tab.
  • Analytics: your choice, which you make when you first open a new installation and can change at any time in the Dogs tab. For installations that predate that question, the Service Provider's legitimate interest in learning which features are used, until you switch it off.
  • Crash reports: the Service Provider's legitimate interest in keeping the Application stable.
  • IP address counters on the proxy: the Service Provider's legitimate interest in preventing abuse and controlling cost.

Is data transferred outside the EU and EEA?

Google, Apple, RevenueCat, Cloudflare and Anthropic are US-based companies, so data sent to them may be processed outside the EU and EEA. They rely on transfer mechanisms such as the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or standard contractual clauses.

What are my rights under the GDPR?

You can ask for access, correction, deletion, restriction and portability, and you can object to processing. Almost all your data is on your phone, where you control it: you can export it as a backup file, change or delete records and dogs in the Application, and remove everything by uninstalling.

For what the proxy, analytics and RevenueCat hold, the Service Provider usually cannot tell which records are yours, because they are tied only to an anonymous ID or an IP address. Write to dogpulse.app@gmail.com and the Service Provider will do what is reasonably possible. You can also complain to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY, imy.se) or to your local data protection authority.

How is your information kept secure?

Since the data you enter is stored locally on your device, its security is primarily managed by your device's own security features. The Service Provider does not store the data you enter on external servers. We recommend keeping your device secured with a PIN, password, or biometric lock. A backup file you export is not encrypted, so store it somewhere only you can access.
