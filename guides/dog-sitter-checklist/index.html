Dog sitter checklist (printable): what to leave and what to tell them

Free to print, about ten minutes to fill in. Updated 25 November 2026.

Handing your dog to a sitter, a friend or a boarding kennel goes better when everything is written down in one place. People remember the feeding times and forget what matters at eleven at night: the vet's phone number, the medication dose, or what to do if the dog will not eat.

Use this page as a dog sitter instructions template. Fill it in on paper or on your phone, tick things off as you go, and leave one printed copy where the sitter will see it.

The short answer

A good dog sitter checklist covers eight things: your contact details and a backup contact, your vet and the nearest emergency clinic, your dog's medications and how to give them, food and meal times, walks and toilet routine, behaviour and house rules, what your dog must never eat, and when to call a vet. Work through the sections below, print a copy and leave it with the sitter.

Things dogs must not eat

Put this list in front of the sitter. If the dog eats any of these, call a vet or a poison helpline straight away. Do not wait for symptoms to show.

  • Chocolate
  • Grapes and raisins
  • Onions, garlic and chives
  • Xylitol, a sweetener found in some chewing gum, sweets and peanut butter
  • Macadamia nuts
  • Alcohol
  • Bones, which can splinter or cause a blockage
  • Medicines meant for people, including ibuprofen and paracetamol

Ask your vet for a fuller list that fits where you live.

When to call a vet

Tell the sitter plainly: if you are unsure, call the vet. Examples of when to call straight away, and to let you know:

  • Repeated vomiting, or not keeping water down
  • Collapse, a seizure or sudden weakness
  • Trouble breathing, or very pale or blueish gums
  • A swollen, hard belly, or retching without bringing anything up
  • Heavy panting, drooling and wobbliness in hot weather
  • A road accident or any serious injury
  • Eating something toxic, or something the sitter cannot identify

This is general guidance, not a diagnosis. A phone call to the vet is always a reasonable answer.

Common questions

What should I tell a dog sitter?

Tell the sitter your contact details and a backup contact, your vet and the nearest emergency clinic, your dog's medications, feeding and walk routine, fears and house rules, what the dog must never eat, and when to call a vet. The checklist above covers each of them.

What information should I leave for a pet sitter?

Leave one printed sheet with contacts, vet and insurance details, the microchip number, a medication table with doses and times, meal times and portions, walk and toilet routine, behaviour notes, house access details and an emergency plan. Add a recent photo of your dog.

What should I leave for a dog sitter?

Leave food for the stay plus two spare days, medicines in their original packaging, a leash and a harness that fits, bowls, poop bags, a bed or blanket, a favourite toy, and a printed copy of this checklist with the vaccination record and the vet's contact card.

How much food should I leave for a dog sitter?

Leave enough for the whole stay plus two spare days, so a delayed return does not become a problem. Measure the portion for the sitter, write down the meal times and leave the measuring cup with the food.

Can a dog sitter take my dog to the vet?

Only if you have told them they may. Decide before you leave whether the sitter can authorise treatment, and the amount you will approve without calling you, then write both down with your vet's and the emergency clinic's details.

What should a dog sitter do in an emergency?

Call the emergency clinic or your vet first, then you or your backup contact. For poisoning, also call an animal poison helpline. Leave the contact numbers on one sheet, and note which symptoms mean call straight away, such as collapse, seizures, trouble breathing or a swollen, hard belly.

Should I meet the dog sitter before I leave?

Yes. A meet and greet lets the sitter and your dog get used to each other, and lets you show the walk route, the food and medicine, and where everything is. Watch how your dog reacts before you commit.

Does a dog sitter need proof of vaccination?

Boarding kennels and some sitters ask for it, so keep the dates ready. Your vet can give you a copy of the vaccination record.
